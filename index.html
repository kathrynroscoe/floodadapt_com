Adaptation isn’t keeping pace with flood risk

Two things slow cities down.

  1. Decisions rest on static reports

    Investment choices are made from expensive consultancy studies that cover only a handful of scenarios. When a new question comes up, there is no quick way to answer it.

  2. Residents don’t feel heard

    When people can’t see how their ideas and concerns were weighed, plans stall or are abandoned altogether.

How it works

A scenario in FloodAdapt is three choices. The platform does the modelling.

  1. Define a what-if

    Combine a weather event, future conditions such as sea level rise or population growth, and an adaptation strategy.

  2. Simulate it

    FloodAdapt runs a rapid, physics-based compound flood model and an impact assessment on every building and road.

  3. See and compare

    Explore flood and impact maps, infographics and metrics, then compare scenarios side by side.

Features

Explore flood scenarios

No modelling background needed. Users create their own scenarios, run them on state-of-the-art models and get visuals they can understand and share.

Unlike viewers preloaded with static maps, FloodAdapt lets you ask new questions and get the specific answers you need to build confidence in a decision and explain it.

Test adaptation strategies

Draw measures on the map and see what they change. Evaluate how well each works and what risk reduction it buys.

Hydraulic
Floodwalls, levees, pumps
Nature-based
Urban greening, storage basins, water squares
Building-level
Floodproofing, elevating homes, relocation

Clear, actionable insights

Customizable infographics show the scale and severity of impacts for each scenario and which parts of the community are hit hardest.

Maps show where measures reduce risk and where residual risk remains, alongside economic metrics such as benefit-cost ratio and internal rate of return.

Set up anywhere. Tuned to your place.

FloodAdapt can be set up quickly for any location using publicly available data.

Local data and knowledge make it sharper: elevated roads that block water, low-income neighbourhoods, protected natural areas. Built in, they give results that people in that place recognise as their own.

From research to an independent company

FloodAdapt was developed at Deltares, the Dutch independent institute for applied research in water and subsurface. The team behind it is now establishing FloodAdapt as a standalone company, to bring the platform to more cities and support them long term.

This site will grow as that happens. If you’d like to use FloodAdapt, partner with us, or follow our progress, we’d like to hear from you.
